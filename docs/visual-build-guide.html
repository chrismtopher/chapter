Chapter Player for Audiobookshelf

Visual build guide

Raspberry Pi Zero 2 W / WH · v5 enclosure

Prepare the Raspberry Pi

  1. In Raspberry Pi Imager, write Raspberry Pi OS Lite. Set hostname chapter-player, username chapter, a password, 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi and its country. Enable SSH.
  2. Insert the card. On a bare Zero 2 W, fit the 40-pin header before wiring. Boot the bare Pi using its own micro-USB PWR IN.
  3. From your computer, connect with ssh chapter@chapter-player.local. On the Pi, run the versioned command in Run the installer.

After installation, run sudo shutdown -h now. Wait for shutdown and unplug the power before wiring.

Fit the inserts and mount the boards

  1. Raspberry Pi Zero 2 W/WH
  2. MAX98357A amplifier
  3. Adafruit 5993 at the rear USB-C opening
  4. Visaton speaker, cone facing the grille
  5. KY-040 under SELECT
  6. KY-040 under VOLUME

Dry-fit the parts first. Install the 18 M2.5 heat-set inserts straight in the mounting bosses. Let them cool, then secure the boards and speaker with M2.5 × 5 mm screws. If a board’s joints will be hard to reach, solder its leads before tightening it down.

Press the four adhesive feet into the bottom recesses. Fit the two printed knobs to their encoder shafts and check that each turns and clicks freely.

Fit the OLED and indicator LED

  1. Slide the OLED up into its window from below. Check its face and orientation from the outside.
  2. Run a small bead of hot glue along the OLED’s bottom edge.
  3. Insert the 3 mm prewired LED from behind and secure it with a dab of hot glue. Keep the glue clear of leads and wire joints.

Keep the cover loose beside the shell while wiring and testing.

Wire the OLED, encoders and amplifier

Unplug power. Orient the Pi like the drawing, with the components facing you, microSD slot at the top and GPIO header on the right. The square pad is physical pin 1. Match the module’s printed labels when attaching wires.

Wire the fused USB-C input

Make the positive and ground splices with the USB-C cable unplugged. Test the harness with the Pi, amp and LED disconnected before plugging in the loads.

  1. Solder the supply leads to VBUS and GND on the 5993. Either matching pad row works. Leave every other pad unused.
  2. Fit the 1.5 A fast-blow, 5 × 20 mm fuse in series with VBUS. After the fuse, branch to Pi physical pin 2, amp VIN and LED +.
  3. Branch USB-C GND to Pi physical pin 20, amp GND and LED −. On the prewired Dioramo 13240, the white-marked lead is positive and the black lead is negative. Insulate the splices.
Meter check before connecting the Pi
  1. USB-C unplugged. With the loads disconnected and the fuse fitted, check for a short between the two harness leads. A steady continuity beep means you should find and fix the short before applying power.
  2. USB-C plugged in, loads still disconnected. Set the meter to DC volts. Red probe in V to the pin 2 lead, black probe in COM to the pin 20 lead. Keep the probe tips apart. The meter should read roughly +5 V. A negative or substantially different reading needs correcting before you connect the Pi.
  3. Unplug USB-C again before attaching the leads. The GPIO feed bypasses the Pi’s input protection. For the assembled player use only the rear USB-C input, with the Pi’s micro-USB PWR IN empty.

The 5993 power path is limited to 1.5 A. If the Pi reports undervoltage or reboots during playback, stop using that power path and check the project’s power guidance.

First boot with the case open

  1. Set the open case on a non-conductive surface and power it from the rear USB-C. Expect the LED to light and the OLED to show chapter during boot.
  2. On your private Wi-Fi, open http://chapter-player.local. If the OLED shows hotspot setup, join Chapter-Setup and open http://10.42.0.1.
  3. Enter the Audiobookshelf server URL and sign in with the dedicated player account. Choose a library and play a book.
If a test fails
  • LED dark. Check the fuse and the voltage at the power harness in step 5. If the Pi boots, check the LED’s polarity and its two branches.
  • LED on, display blank. The LED gets power before the Pi boots. Give the Pi time to start, then check the OLED’s feed from Pi pin 1, ground and SPI wires against step 4.
  • Knob turns the wrong way or fails to click. Check CLK and DT against the diagram for that knob. Check SW and ground if pressing it does nothing.
  • Silent speaker. Check mute and volume, then compare the amp’s three I²S leads, both supply leads and both speaker leads with steps 4 and 5.

Shut down and disconnect USB-C before moving any wire.

Snap the front cover into place

  1. Once the open-case tests pass, shut down and unplug. Tuck the wiring away from the rim and snap tabs.
  2. Align the cover and hook in one side first. Press gently around the perimeter until the remaining tabs engage.
  3. Check for an even seam. If a tab resists, stop and check the alignment and wiring. Power up and replay a book with the case closed.

Check the complete wiring

Start at the rear USB-C socket, follow the two power rails, then compare each occupied Pi header position with its labelled lead. The speaker has its own pair at the amplifier.
